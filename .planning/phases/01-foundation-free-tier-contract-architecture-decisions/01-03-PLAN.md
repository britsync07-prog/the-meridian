<objective>
Create the versioned free-tier ceiling registry, the typed accessor that is the only legal home for a ceiling value, and the standing written decision rule that resolves the tension between "the paid plan is bought at three triggers" and "the project never spends".

Purpose: The free tier is not a budget with an escape hatch — it is a permanent architectural constraint (D-05). That makes the ceilings a design input rather than an operational afterthought: every ceiling has to be known, sourced and dated before the code that depends on it is written, and the rule that decides what happens when a ceiling is approached has to live in the same file as the budget so the two can never disagree. OPS-01 and OPS-04 demand exactly that, and the enforcement test turns "every ceiling is in one place" from an aspiration into a gate that fails the build.

Output: `config/free-tier-limits.json` holding sixteen ceilings plus the decision rule; `shared/config/free-tier-limits.ts` as the single accessor; a test that proves no other code path reads a ceiling, and proves it can fail; and the ADR recording the standing no-spend disposition.
</objective>

The ADR must record, in this order:

1. **Context.** The free tier is the project's design constraint, not a temporary budget. The reconciled ceilings live in `config/free-tier-limits.json` with their source URLs and verification dates (OPS-01). The two limits the project will actually hit are the Workers request ceiling (~5,000-7,000 daily visitors against a 1,000-visitor target) and R2 storage (~month 14).

2. **Decision.** D-05: the free tier is a **permanent hard ceiling**. The project never buys Workers Paid ($5/month) or any other paid tier, **not even once the site earns revenue**. Revenue is not a licence to spend on infrastructure.

3. **The OPS-04 triggers and their response.** Name all three triggers explicitly, with the metric and the threshold, and give each its response:
   - daily visitors approaching 4,000 → promote hot stories into the static tier (D-07)
   - a static project exceeding 20,000 files → shard the static tier across the 8 projects (D-06, D-07)
   - any revenue → still **no purchase** (D-05); the response is the same load shedding
   The ADR must state that the rule satisfies OPS-04 by NAMING all three triggers, while the standing disposition for each is load shedding rather than spending.

4. **D-07 and D-08.** All three load-shedding mechanisms act together: promoting hot stories into the static tier (served free, unlimited requests), edge-caching the story tier hard, and sharding the static tier across the 8 projects. The request ceiling is a **permanent traffic wall**, not a tripwire; crossing it requires the static promotion path, not a spend decision. The story tier may never be the only tier.

5. **The honest limits of Cloudflare's alerting.** Cloudflare Budget Alerts are **informational only** — Cloudflare's own documentation states the alert "does not cap your usage or impact your account in any way", and usage is processed once per day for the prior day's activity, so an alert fires the day after the threshold is reached rather than in real time. Budget alerts are also spend-THRESHOLD based, not percentage-of-quota based. Therefore:
   - the **lifecycle rules** (plan 01-04) are the real cap on storage growth, because they delete objects and so keep GB-months and Class A/B ops inside the free allowance by construction;
   - the **50% and 75% percentage-of-ceiling alerts** are the audit Worker's job in Phase 7 (OPS-03), reading this same registry;
   - the budget alerts configured in the Cloudflare dashboard cover the dollar-metered products as a notification, not as a control.
   This section MUST NOT claim that alerts prevent billing.

6. **D-09.** Open-Meteo's free tier is restricted to non-commercial use and requires CC BY 4.0 attribution. The project serves it unchanged in v1 and carries a standing obligation to source a free, commercially-usable replacement **before any revenue exists**. It will not buy the Open-Meteo paid Standard plan at monetisation; the replacement is the only acceptable path.
